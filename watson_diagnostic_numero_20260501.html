Section 02

Issues Found

Issue #1
The Headline Describes the Delivery, Not the Desire
"Numero — Outsourced Finance Department. End-to-end financial services: accounting, analysis, and strategy — delivered by a team with 25+ years of experience."

This tells the buyer what they're buying. It doesn't tell them what changes in their life when they buy it. The founder arriving at this page doesn't want an outsourced finance department. They want to stop being afraid every time someone asks them about their margins. The headline is talking about the product. It should be talking about the transformation.

Trigger affected:Emotional Trigger Strength + Buyer Language Accuracy
Issue #2
The Case Studies Strip Out the Proof That Would Actually Sell
"01 Manufacturing Company — We implemented a management accounting system and trained the internal team on ongoing data supervision."

That tells me what Numero did. It doesn't tell me what the founder felt when they finally understood their numbers, or what decision they made that they couldn't make before. The prospect reading this is looking for someone who's solved their specific problem. Generic industry labels and process descriptions don't create that recognition. Real outcomes with real numbers do.

Trigger affected:Trust Signal Density + Psychological Tripwire Activation
Issue #3
The "Financial Clarity" Product Launch Has No Psychological Hook
"I'm currently launching a new financial clarity product designed for founders who want to confidently work with their P&L and CashFlow without becoming finance experts themselves."

The idea is strong. The copy is thin. "Confidently work with your P&L" is still founder language. The buyer's version sounds more like: "Finally understand whether you can afford to hire that person without having a panic attack about it." The early-50%-off offer is buried after a paragraph of credentials. The offer needs its own page, its own psychology, and its own copy that speaks the buyer's fear back to them before asking for commitment.

Kamila has something most people who land on this sort of homepage don't: real results, real retention numbers, and a genuinely useful service. That's the good news.

The bad news is that none of it comes through in the copy. The homepage reads like it was written to reassure a bank, not a founder who's terrified their business is bleeding cash they can't see. It's accurate. It's professional. And cold traffic won't give it a second glance.

The "Financial Clarity" product is the real opportunity here. The idea is excellent. The concept of helping founders understand their own numbers without becoming accountants is exactly what the market wants.
