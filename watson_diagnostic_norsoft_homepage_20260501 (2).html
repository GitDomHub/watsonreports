Section 02

Issues Found

Issue #1
The Headline Talks About Norsoft, Not the Buyer
"Modern Systems, Old School Reliability"

Your buyer isn't thinking about your reliability. They're thinking about their problem: being stuck with software that costs too much, breaks during critical moments, or does 60% of what the business actually needs. This headline tells them what you believe about yourselves. That's not why they came. They came because something is broken.

Trigger affected:Emotional Trigger Strength
Issue #2
Service Names Are Developer Language, Not Buyer Language
"Legacy System Modernisation · Custom ERP Solutions · Cloud Service & Migration · IT Consulting & Strategy"

Your buyer doesn't wake up thinking "I need legacy system modernisation." They wake up thinking "half my team is manually re-entering data between two systems that don't talk to each other, and my CFO is asking why IT costs keep climbing." When your service names sound like a technical dictionary, the buyer can't connect what you do to the pain they're feeling.

Trigger affected:Buyer Language Accuracy + Identity Confirmation
Issue #3
The CTAs Have No Stakes
"Start Your Project · Explore Our · Have An Idea to Discuss?"

These ask the buyer to take action without giving them a reason to do it now. A buyer with a real problem needs to feel that delay is costing them. "Start Your Project" is a polite invitation. It doesn't acknowledge what they're losing every month by staying on the current system. No cost of waiting, no consequence of inaction, no urgency.

Trigger affected:Urgency / Consequence Framing
Section 03

Watson's Notes

Watson
Copy Diagnostician  •  Just Digital

The bones here are fine. The design is clean. There's clearly a team who knows what they're doing. But the copy reads like it was written for a case study submission, not for a business owner who's been burned by overpriced SaaS, or who's lost three months waiting for a freelancer to deliver something that broke in production.

That SaaS escape calculator is the most interesting thing on the page. A $590K projected saving is a number someone actually feels. It should be above the fold. It should be the headline, frankly. Instead it's buried in the middle section, surrounded by bullet points about ERP migration.

The "Full-Stack Expertise" section with the glowing brain illustration is doing some heavy lifting visually. But the copy underneath it? "Full-stack expertise." That's not copy. That's a category. Sherlock would have a field day with this one.

I've flagged the obvious. The psychological archaeology (the exact words a skeptical CTO uses at 11pm when they finally admit they have a problem) is the other fellow's department.
