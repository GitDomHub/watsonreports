7 Psychological Buying Triggers

Emotional Trigger Strength

The mission ("money with a mission") creates a warm feeling. But warm feelings don't convert. There's no fear of missing out, no friction about the status quo, no gut-punch about what's wrong with regular investing right now.

Buyer Language Accuracy
2/5
🔴 Critical

The page speaks the language of someone who built iNaam, not someone wondering if they can actually afford to invest ethically. Terms like "impact-aligned businesses" and "learn before you earn" are founder-first framing. Real buyers think in different words entirely.

Pain Point Clarity
2/5
🔴 Critical

What problem does iNaam actually solve? The page gestures at "doing good doesn't mean doing worse" — but this assumes the visitor already knows what the old way costs them. They don't. The pain is assumed, not named.

Identity Confirmation
2/5
🔴 Critical

There's a section called "not sure what kind of investor you are?" — which is well-intentioned but accidentally tells the visitor they don't know themselves yet. Good copy makes people feel seen, not uncertain.

Psychological Tripwire Activation
3/5
🟡 Weak

The $50,000 and $20 stats are genuinely compelling. But they're buried mid-page, stripped of emotional context. Numbers without stakes are just numbers. What does $50,000 starting amount mean for someone who only has $500?

Trust Signal Density
3/5
🟡 Weak

Testimonials are present but small and hard to read at scroll speed. The comparison table against competitors is a solid move — but it's positioned too late. Trust needs to arrive before the visitor's scepticism does.

Urgency / Consequence Framing
1/5

There is no reason to download the app today rather than tomorrow, or next month, or never. No consequence of waiting. No cost of staying with what they've got. The CTAs exist. The reason to click them now does not.

Section 02

Issues Found

Issue #1
The Headline Promises a Feeling, Not an Outcome
"money with a mission"

This is a brand statement, not a buyer statement. It tells someone what iNaam stands for. It doesn't tell them what they get. Your buyer's first question isn't "do these people have a mission?" It's "is this for someone like me, and will it actually work?" Four words is all you get. These four aren't answering either question.

Trigger affected:Buyer Language Accuracy
Issue #2
The Pain Lives Between the Lines — Not on the Page
"doing good doesn't mean doing worse"

This is a rebuttal to an objection nobody voiced out loud. The visitor hasn't said "I'm worried ethical investing means lower returns" — you've assumed they have that worry, and you're quietly defending against it. That's the wrong order. Name the frustration first. Then resolve it. Right now, most visitors will read this as a general motivational statement and scroll past it without registering the actual claim.

Trigger affected:Pain Point Clarity
Issue #3
Zero Cost of Inaction Anywhere on the Page
"not sure what kind of investor you are?"

This section is meant to be helpful, but it's accidentally stalling the decision. You've positioned iNaam as something the visitor needs to figure themselves out first before they're ready for. That's the opposite of what a strong CTA does. A strong CTA removes the feeling that there's a prerequisite. This section creates one. The visitor who was almost ready to download now thinks "I should figure out my investor type first" — and they close the tab to think about it. They don't come back.

Trigger affected:Urgency / Consequence Framing + Identity Confirmation
Section 03

Watson's Notes

Watson
Copy Diagnostician · Just Digital

Right. iNaam has a genuinely interesting product and a mission that, I suspect, a lot of people would get behind if they felt it was for them. That's the problem. They don't feel that yet.

The page is built around what the company believes. Not around what the visitor is quietly afraid of. They're worried their money isn't working hard enough. They're suspicious that "ethical" is a shorthand for "worse returns." They want to feel like a smart person, not a good person. Nobody queues for the moral high ground when the interest rate is lower.

Elementary problems, really. The bones are strong. The psychology is missing. Rather like having a lovely door with no handle.
